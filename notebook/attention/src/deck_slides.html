Attention, blocked

FlashAttention computes exactly this — the same multiplies, the same exps — without ever storing the score matrix. This deck builds it up from the textbook formula, one idea per slide.

Three steps, one head

Scores. Every query row against every key: S = Q Kᵀ / √d. Q is Tq × d, K is Tkv × d, so S is Tq × Tkv.

Probabilities. Each row of S through a softmax: subtract the row's max, exponentiate, divide by the row's sum.

Mix. O = P V: each output row is a probability-weighted mix of the value rows. O is Tq × d, like Q.

Sizes in a real model: Tq = tokens processed now (the prompt in prefill, 2k–8k; 1 per step in decode) · Tkv = tokens they can look at (the context, growing by one per generated token) · d = one head's width, 64–128 · many heads side by side (Llama-3-8B: 32 × 128 = 4096) · a batch repeats it per sequence.

Both S = QKᵀ and O = PV are matmuls — the ones the matmul deck rearranges. What is new is the softmax between them.

Done the obvious way

One query row per step: its scores go to memory, come back for the softmax, the probabilities go to memory, come back for O. Watch the violet counters.

Softmax needs the whole row

A softmax divides by the sum of its row, after subtracting the row's max. Neither is known until every score of the row exists.

So plain attention keeps all of S: Tq × Tkv numbers, then as many probabilities. At 4096 tokens that is 16.8 million scores per head — memory traffic, not arithmetic, sets the time.

The fix: do not wait for the row. Keep a running max and a running sum.

Online softmax, one key at a time

Keep, per query row, m (the largest score so far) and l (the sum of exp(score − m) so far), and O unnormalised.

A new score s: m′ = max(m, s). Everything kept so far was weighted with e−m, so multiply l and O by em−m′, then add the new term.

At the end, O = O / l — exactly the softmax. Nothing was stored but m, l and O.

Keys in blocks of Bc

Take keys Bc at a time: one small matmul for the scores, one softmax update, one small matmul into O. The rescale happens once per block after the first — bigger blocks, fewer rescales, a bigger S block on chip. bwz attention --bc.

Queries in blocks of Br: programs

A program owns Br rows of Q and their O, and streams every key block past them. Each block of S is Br × Bc and never leaves the core. bwz attention --br.

Programs on cores, in waves

Programs share only K and V, so they run on different cores at once, dealt round-robin: core c takes program wave · cores + c. Every busy core does the same phase on its own rows.

Inside a block: S = QiKjᵀ is a matmul

M = Br, K = d, N = Bc — drawn by the matmul deck's own view. os keeps a block of S and sweeps d inside it; ws and is cut d over the grid and leave pieces to add. bwz attention --stationarity.

What the inner dataflow costs

On a GPU tensor core there is no accumulator that keeps K's pieces in place: ws and is leave (slices − 1) · M · N partial sums per inner matmul for the vector unit. os leaves none.

S = QKᵀ is cut along d; O += PV is cut along Bc. A bigger Bc means fewer rescales but, under ws, more pieces of P·V to add.

bwz attention costs every block size under every dataflow the unit declares and keeps the cheapest — on A100 that is os, on Metis ws, whose periphery adds the pieces for free.

Heads and batch: more programs, nothing shared

Every (batch, head) has its own Q, K and V, so both simply multiply the programs. Unlike a projection, where a batch reuses the weights, nothing is reused here: K and V bytes grow with batch × heads.

A head across two waves reads K and V twice

K and V are fetched once per wave and shared by the programs of that head in the wave. 3 heads × 3 programs on 4 cores: heads 1 and 2 straddle a wave boundary, so their K and V cross memory twice — the magenta counter.
